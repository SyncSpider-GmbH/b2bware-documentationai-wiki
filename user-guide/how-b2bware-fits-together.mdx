B2Bware Admin is not a list of unrelated menus. **Products**, **Orders**, and **Customers** — together with **Auth** and **Settings** — form the commerce core that powers catalog, pricing, who can buy, and how orders are placed.

<Callout kind="info" collapsed="false">
This page explains *why* the hubs exist and how they connect. For day-to-day clicks in Products, see the Products how-tos. For Orders and Customers, start with their overview pages linked below.
</Callout>

## The storefront spine

In the platform, a tenant is treated as ready for storefront-style commerce when these apps are installed together:

| Piece | Role in the spine |
| --- | --- |
| **Products** | What can be sold — catalog, stock locations, prices and pricelists, vendors |
| **Orders** | How money moves — carts, checkout, sales orders, proposals/quotes |
| **Customers** | Who can buy and who can operate Admin — customers, companies, contacts, roles, segmentation (customer groups) |
| **Auth** | Identity for shop users and Admin operators (no separate sidebar label) |
| **Settings** | Tenant configuration — general, system, translations, and related setup |

Optional hubs (Attributes, Rules, Tax, Media, Loyalty, Invoices, and others) **stack on top** of this spine. They enrich the core; they are not required for every tenant.

## How the pieces depend on each other

1. **Customers** define *who* may buy and *who* may open Admin (roles and permissions). **Segmentation** in the sidebar is the UI name for **customer groups** — used for group prices, permissions, and checkout behaviour.
2. **Products** define *what* can be sold. Live prices can include regular, special, tier (quantity), group (customer group), and cost. **Pricelists** stage bulk price changes and only become live when you **deploy** them.
3. **Orders** turn catalog + identity into transactions: cart → checkout → order. **Proposals** (quotes) support B2B quoting before a firm order. Trusted unit prices are resolved **on the server** so the price a buyer sees matches what is charged.
4. Optional hubs adjust or gate outcomes: **Attributes** extend products and checkout fields; **Rules** apply catalog/cart promotions on top of base prices; Tax, Invoices, Loyalty, and similar hubs attach when installed.

Admin often opens on **Orders** as the operational home for daily commerce work.

## Why a hub might be missing from the sidebar

A sidebar item appears only when roughly all of these are true:

- The hub (app) is **installed** for the tenant
- The signed-in operator has a matching **view** permission for that hub
- For Store Setup / Mobile App specifically, the full storefront spine combination is installed

<Callout kind="tip" collapsed="false">
If a colleague sees a hub you do not, compare **installed apps** for the tenant and **role permissions** — not only the menu layout.
</Callout>

## Core vs optional (for documentation and training)

| Core commerce spine | Common optionals (install-gated) |
| --- | --- |
| Products, Orders, Customers, Auth, Settings | Attributes, Rules, Tax, Media, Notifications, Loyalty, Invoices, Procurement, Accounting, Storefront Builder, Automation, Integrations, … |

Do **not** document optional modules as always present. Say “if this hub is installed for your tenant.”

## Do not assume

- Sidebar visibility alone is not security — permissions still gate actions.
- Shop-facing permission labels may exist in the platform without being fully enforced yet; many buyer journeys are still driven by **tenant settings** (for example login required for prices, guest checkout flags).
- Social or marketing hubs are **not** part of this spine and are out of scope for this guide until they ship on Production for your tenant.
